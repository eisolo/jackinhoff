How a session moves

Three rooms, one afternoon

01

Under the floor

Most of a day runs on habits you did not pick this morning. We start there. I use a muscle check, the way the BodyTalk system teaches it, and I follow whatever your body points at. I am the translator. Your body is the one who knows the order.

02

The part you can say

I take what you tell me and what is already moving in the room, and I let it sit until a clear sentence shows up. Then I say it. People tell me the sentence keeps working after we hang up.

03

The week after

You will wobble between the old loop and the new one at the sink, in the car, on a call with your brother. That wobble is the work. You practice the new line while life is still ordinary.

The pace of the work is listen, sort, clear, practice, stay.

The tools

What we actually use

Body check

Dr. John Veltheim built BodyTalk on a simple bet. A living body can point at its own next repair better than a gadget can. We use a map of body, mood, and story, and a muscle check to ask which piece is first.

You are already one version of yourself. The check is how we pick a cleaner one and give the body a chance to catch up.

Human Design

Ra Uru Hu's chart. Astrology, the I Ching, the chakras, the tree of life, and a little modern physics, drawn as a picture of how you are built to meet a day.

I use yours so we stop asking you to work like the person next to you. If a theme on the chart is stuck, we take it to the body check instead of talking it to death.

Gene Keys

Richard Rudd's contemplation of 64 archetypes, lined up with the I Ching. You carry a specific handful. I know mine well enough to speak from them, and I use that, plus the check, when yours is stuck in the shadow version.

This is not a scripted reading. You leave with your chart if you want to keep looking on your own.

Direct, a little playful, and I narrate what I am seeing while I see it.
